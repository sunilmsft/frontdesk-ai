2. Brand DNA

▾

Philosophy

The SMB Owner Is the Hero. We are invisible infrastructure. Every site makes them look good. Their name is the headline, their photo builds trust, their words tell the story. "Powered by WelcomeMat" stays in the footer — subtle, never competing.

Confidence Over Complexity. SMB owners are busy, often non-technical, sometimes in a second language. Fewer options, better defaults. Show the result, not the process. One clear next step, not a decision tree.

Human-First AI. The AI assistant is a helpful front desk employee, not a chatbot. Never announces itself as AI. Responds in customer's language automatically. Knows the business deeply. Escalates gracefully. Never fabricates.

Perceived Uniqueness > Architectural Uniqueness. Every site feels custom. Under the hood, 90%+ is shared.

Voice & Tone

Warm but not cutesy. Professional but not corporate. Bilingual-friendly.

  • First person for the business owner: "I offer...", "We care about..."
  • Never say "custom-designed" — say "built around your business"
  • Never say "AI-powered" to end customers — let it just work
  • Trust language uses proof: "5+ years experience" — not "best in class"
  • CTAs are invitations: "Get a Free Estimate" not "BUY NOW"

Language Principles

  • English is the authoring language; translations generated from EN
  • Portuguese and Spanish are first-class — not afterthoughts
  • All visible text gets data-i18n attributes from the start
  • Widget greetings translate with page language

4. Site Building Playbook

▾

Pre-Build Checklist

Assets: Logo (high-res PNG/SVG), owner photo, 6-10 work photos (renamed descriptively), business card/brand guide
Info (one pass): Business name, owner name, phone, email, services + descriptions, areas, hours, languages, color preference, tagline
Create DB record FIRST (slug, system_prompt, welcome_message, theme_color)
Set up folders: public/sites/{slug}/photos/ and logo/

Design Decisions That Work

  • Fonts: Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans (premium). Alt: Playfair Display + Inter
  • Colors: Dark primary + warm accent (champagne/gold universal) + off-white bg (#fafaf8)
  • Shadows: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.07), 0 8px 24px rgba(0,0,0,0.05) minimum
  • Section labels: Accent-colored, uppercase, letter-spacing: 3px
  • Icons: Lucide ONLY via CDN. Never emojis for structural elements.
  • Dark hero with gradient: Works across all verticals

Mistakes to Avoid (Repeat Offenders)

Nav: MUST stay dark if hero is dark. Never transition to white bg on scroll. Use dark frosted glass.
Cards (CRITICAL): NEVER same-color card bg on same-color section bg. Alternating pattern: tinted section + white cards → white section + tinted cards. This bug happened on BOTH Camellia and pitch page.
Logo: Never thin-line logos at small sizes. Always display: block. Use circular frame for non-square logos.
Language picker: Dropdown with full names + flags, not abbreviation toggles. Place in nav bar.
About section: Use owner's actual words. Don't over-polish into corporate speak.

Build Process (Phase 0 — Current)

  1. Copy public/sites/camellia-cleaning/ as starting point
  2. Find-replace: business name, slug, phone, email, areas, hours
  3. Update photos, logo, colors, services, translations
  4. Create DB record with system_prompt + welcome_message
  5. Test: page load, nav links, language switching, chatbot, mobile
  6. Share preview URL → get approval → set up custom domain

Deployment Flow

  1. Local → test at localhost:3001/site/{slug}
  2. Commit + push to GitHub → Render auto-deploys from master
  3. Share preview link
  4. Approved → buy domain → point DNS to Render → add custom domain in Render

Templatization Phases

PhaseTriggerApproach
Phase 01-3 customersManual copy-from-reference. Learning > efficiency.
Phase 13-10 customersConfig-driven template + JSON config per business. Required.
Phase 210-25 customersAdmin-editable content. Nice-to-have.
Phase 325+ customersSelf-serve onboarding with AI-generated sites. Aspirational.
Don't skip phases. Each triggered by real pain, not anticipated need.

5. GTM & Outreach Strategy

▾
Established May 21, 2026. Strategy grounded in current state: 1 customer in preview, 1 warm lead, zero paying customers.

Core Principle

Close the first loop before building systems. Convert Kesia from free preview → paying customer. That's the real validation. No outreach system, demo library, or vertical strategy matters until someone has paid and stayed.

The WelcomeMat Sales Motion

"Show, don't pitch." Most web agencies send a PDF proposal. We send a live preview. That's the real differentiator.

"I saw your Google reviews — your customers love you. I help businesses like yours look as good online as they do in person."

No mention of AI, websites, widgets, or technology.

The Kesia Flywheel

  1. Kesia picks domain → go live → she's thrilled
  2. Ask: "Would any friends in the cleaning groups want something like this?"
  3. She shares her site in WhatsApp group with personal endorsement
  4. 2-3 respond → build their sites → they share in their networks
  5. 5-6 customers in cleaning, all warm referrals
This is how SMB services actually grow. Not through systems. Through one happy customer telling three friends.

Demo Showcase Strategy

  • One real customer site > ten demo sites

Lead Discovery (Google Maps)

  1. Search Google Maps for [service type] in [area]
  2. Filter: 4+ stars, 20+ reviews, no website or outdated
  3. Screenshot their listing — that's your prospect card
  4. Note name, phone, review count, what customers praise
Lead discovery is NOT the bottleneck. The bottleneck is: getting them to respond → converting → delivering → retaining.

11. Drift Protection

▾

Warning Signals

Custom Agency Drift

Signal: Spending more than 2 hours building a new customer's site.

Fix: Content collection → fix onboarding. Design → make it a knob. Truly custom → charge or decline.

Feature Bloat

Signal: Adding features no current customer asked for.

Fix: Every feature traces to a real request or measurable conversion improvement.

Operational Overload

Signal: Spending more time maintaining than building.

Fix: Template system too fragile → invest in shared infra before adding customers.

Generic Template Flattening

Signal: Two customers side-by-side look obviously identical.

Fix: Different photos, color, fonts, owner story.

Over-Corporate Enterprise UX

Signal: Site feels designed for a SaaS company, not a house cleaner.

Fix: Serif fonts, warm colors, personal photos, first-person copy, "call me" CTAs.

Anti-Patterns

  • "Just this once" customization — make it a knob or don't do it
  • Scope creep on updates — max 2 content changes/mo. Not redesigns.
  • Treating sites as projects — it's a configuration. Project feel → agency drift.
  • Building admin tools before 10 customers — spreadsheets are fine until they're not
  • Optimizing for edge cases — build for the 80% (service biz, 1 location, 3-6 services)

Maintenance Burden Watchlist

After Each Site Build — Answer These

  • What new reusable patterns emerged?
  • What was unexpectedly manual?
  • Did any "never customize" items get customized? Why?
  • What new knobs would have saved time?
  • Did the customer notice template similarity?
  • What trust patterns worked best for this vertical?
  • What onboarding friction could be eliminated?
