# WelcomeMat Concept Candidates

> Candidate-readiness briefs for the WelcomeMat concept intake. These are planning documents, not approved website content or implementation specifications.

## Working Principles

- Archetypes are starting structures, not finished templates.
- Known facts, working hypotheses, and unanswered questions are kept separate.
- Do not invent menus, events, testimonials, prices, images, metrics, customer claims, or operational capabilities.
- Concept work begins only after the owner submits the intake and the missing facts and materials are confirmed.

## Candidate 1: Annapoorna Gharacha Swaad

**Working archetype:** `Home Food & Catering`

### What Is Known

- Owner/contact: Pallavi Kamat.
- Offers home-style Maharashtrian and North Indian food.
- Operates from a commercial kitchen.
- Food can be customized to customer requirements.
- Facebook page: Annapoorna Gharacha Swaad.
- Interest originated from the WelcomeMat Facebook experiment.

### What Remains Unknown

- Exact menu, signature dishes, seasonal availability, and portion or serving formats.
- Whether the business accepts party, pooja, corporate, or other catering orders.
- Pickup, delivery, service area, delivery fees, and available pickup windows.
- Preferred ordering channel, required lead time, minimum order size, and payment process.
- Dietary options, allergen handling, spice-level choices, and customization limits.
- Kitchen licensing or other customer-facing compliance information that should be displayed.
- Food photography, branding, reviews, and permission to reuse Facebook content.
- Languages supported for customer conversations and ordering.

### Follow-up Questions

1. What are the current menu categories and the dishes customers ask for most often?
2. Which orders do you want to prioritize: individual meals, party trays, pooja orders, or broader catering?
3. Where do you offer pickup or delivery, and how much lead time is normally needed?
4. How should a customer place an order or request a custom order: call, WhatsApp, Facebook, or another channel?
5. What dietary, allergen, spice, ingredient, or portion questions should the assistant answer, and which should be referred to you?
6. Which languages should be supported, and who will review translated food and ordering information?
7. What photos, menu files, reviews, logo assets, and social links may WelcomeMat use?

### Likely Customer Journeys

- Browse food categories or signature dishes, then ask about availability and serving size.
- Plan a party or pooja order, provide date, guest count, preferences, and location, then request a quote.
- Ask whether a dish can be customized for dietary needs, spice preferences, or ingredients.
- Confirm pickup or delivery coverage, lead time, ordering channel, and next step.
- View food images or approved reviews, then call, message, or submit an inquiry.

These are working hypotheses to validate, not claims that these services are currently offered.

### Likely Concept Components

- Clear food and catering positioning based on approved owner language.
- Menu or dish categories using owner-provided information only.
- A catering/custom-order inquiry flow that collects date, guest count, needs, service area, and contact details if approved.
- Food gallery using owner-provided images with permission.
- Approved customer reviews, if supplied and authorized.
- Multilingual assistant grounded in the approved menu, ordering, and service-area content.
- Prominent approved actions such as call, WhatsApp, Facebook, or inquiry.

### Materials Needed Before Concept Creation

- Current menu, dish descriptions, prices or quote guidance if the owner wants them shown, and availability rules.
- Catering/package information, order minimums, lead times, pickup/delivery details, and service area.
- Logo, colors, food photographs, and permission for each asset.
- Review text with source/permission, if reviews are included.
- Preferred phone, WhatsApp, Facebook, or inquiry destination.
- Dietary/allergen/customization guidance and escalation rules.
- Approved languages and reviewed translations where accuracy matters.

### Risks and Assumptions

- Do not assume a home-style offering means home-based preparation; the known operating detail is a commercial kitchen.
- Do not assume delivery, pickup, party or pooja catering, pricing, payment methods, licensing, or dietary certifications.
- Do not present dishes, availability, reviews, images, service areas, or ordering channels until confirmed.
- Do not make medical, allergen-safety, freshness, or cultural-authenticity claims without owner-approved wording.
- Facebook content is a lead and reference source, not automatically reusable website content.

### Suggested Minimum Viable Concept Scope

Start with a focused mobile experience that explains the food offering, shows only an approved short menu or category set, and provides one clear inquiry path. Include a lightweight catering/custom-order form with date, guest count, location, dietary/customization notes, and contact details only after the owner confirms those fields. Add a small approved gallery and multilingual assistant only if source material and review ownership are ready. Keep unconfirmed ordering, delivery, pricing, and compliance details out of the first concept.

## Candidate 2: Macaroni KID Snoqualmie Valley & Issaquah

**Working archetype:** `Community Discovery & Local Publisher`

### What Is Known

- Contact: Annmarie Mitchell.
- Helps families discover local fun hosted by local businesses.
- Existing website: https://snoqualmievalley.macaronikid.com/
- It is part of the broader Macaroni KID publishing platform.
- Interest originated from the WelcomeMat Facebook experiment.

### What Remains Unknown

- The intended audience split among parents, caregivers, children, local businesses, and community partners.
- Geographic coverage and how Snoqualmie Valley and Issaquah are defined for discovery.
- Event data sources, editorial ownership, approval rules, expiration rules, and update cadence.
- Whether WelcomeMat may access, copy, transform, or display existing platform content.
- Available APIs, exports, feeds, embed options, or platform-approved integration methods.
- Publisher workflow for daily add, edit, schedule, approve, archive, and feature actions.
- Newsletter, advertising, featured-business, sponsorship, and inquiry workflows.
- Accessibility, safety, age guidance, venue details, cost, registration, and cancellation fields.
- Which languages and translation review process are appropriate.

### Follow-up Questions

1. What are the highest-value family searches: today, this weekend, free, age range, location, indoor/outdoor, or another priority?
2. Who owns event creation, fact checking, approval, edits, recurring schedules, and expiration?
3. What is the authoritative source for each event, and how frequently should the concept refresh it?
4. Which event fields are mandatory, and what happens when cost, age, venue, registration, or cancellation details are unknown?
5. How should local businesses submit events or advertising inquiries, and what approval steps are required?
6. Which articles, family guides, featured businesses, and newsletter content may be included or linked?
7. What does the Macaroni KID platform or franchise agreement permit for companion experiences, data use, branding, and integrations?
8. Should the concept complement the current website, or is there a specific workflow it should improve without replacing the publisher site?
9. Which languages are useful, and who approves translated event or safety information?

### Likely Family-Facing Journeys

- Find activities for today or this weekend by location, age, cost, and indoor/outdoor preference.
- Ask a natural-language question such as what is free or suitable for a particular age range.
- Open an event, review date, time, location, cost, registration, age guidance, and source link, then continue to the approved destination.
- Discover recurring events while seeing the next valid occurrence rather than an expired listing.
- Browse family guides, articles, or featured local businesses from approved publisher content.

### Likely Publisher/Admin Journeys

- Add or import an event, validate required fields, schedule publication, and approve it.
- Edit a changed event, update a recurring series, and archive or expire an event safely.
- Review event submissions and publisher notes before content becomes searchable.
- Feature an approved event, article, guide, or business for a defined period.
- Receive and route newsletter, advertiser, sponsorship, or business inquiries.
- Audit the source and last-reviewed date for content used by the assistant.

### Materials and Sample Data Needed

- Approved sample events covering one-time and recurring events, expired events, free and paid events, and indoor/outdoor cases.
- Event schema and required fields, including source URL, age guidance, cost, registration, venue, cancellation, and last-reviewed date.
- Editorial calendar, approval states, archive rules, and examples of publisher/admin workflows.
- Approved articles, family guides, featured-business content, newsletter examples, and advertising inquiry routing.
- Platform documentation or written permission covering feeds, exports, APIs, embeds, content reuse, branding, and franchise controls.
- Accessibility, safety, privacy, and translation guidance.

### Platform-Control and Franchise Constraints

- Confirm whether Macaroni KID centrally controls the platform, data, brand, templates, domains, integrations, or content rights.
- Confirm whether a WelcomeMat concept may read from or link to the existing site without copying content.
- Confirm who owns submitted event data, images, articles, reviews, and advertiser information.
- Confirm whether the concept can authenticate to an admin workflow, use a feed/API, or must remain a manually maintained companion.
- Treat all integration and content permissions as unanswered until Annmarie or the platform owner confirms them.

### Risks and Assumptions

- Do not claim complete event coverage, real-time freshness, automatic expiration, or publisher approval unless the data source and workflow support it.
- Do not invent events, prices, ages, venues, registration links, testimonials, audience metrics, or advertiser results.
- Do not scrape, reproduce, translate, or train against platform content without clear permission.
- Do not imply that a companion experience replaces the Macaroni KID website or platform.
- The assistant must answer only from approved, current content and should link or escalate when information is missing or time-sensitive.
- Safety, cost, availability, cancellation, and age guidance require source-backed handling.

### Suggested Minimum Viable Concept Scope

Begin with a companion discovery layer over a small, explicitly approved sample dataset or feed. Support search for date, area, age, cost, and indoor/outdoor preference, plus “today,” “this weekend,” and “free” collections only when the source data supports them. Show source links, last-reviewed dates, and clear unknown states. Include a simple publisher review surface or documented manual workflow for add, edit, approve, schedule, and archive. Defer broad content ingestion, automatic recurring-event logic, newsletters, advertising workflows, multilingual output, and any replacement-site positioning until platform permissions, source data, and ownership are confirmed.
