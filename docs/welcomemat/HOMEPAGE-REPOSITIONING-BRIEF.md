# WelcomeMat Digital Homepage Repositioning Brief v1.1

**Status:** Approved for Phase 1 implementation  
**Date:** August 20, 2026  
**Planning scope:** Homepage and homepage assistant only. No implementation is included in this brief.

## Baseline and decision frame

The verified production homepage is the current `public/index.html`. It leads with helping local pros get found and answer customers, then presents FrontDesk AI and Swoop as two product cards with fixed or early-access language. The Concept intake is live at `/concept` and already asks owners about their business, service area, desired customer actions, the experience they want to improve, useful languages, and available materials.

Use the formal brand name **WelcomeMat Digital** in formal references and headings. Use **WelcomeMat** as conversational shorthand in body copy. The domain is `welcomematdigital.com`. The customer problem may involve calls, follow-up, communication, booking, inquiries, or other experiences; it is not limited to websites or explicitly digital problems.

The repositioning should preserve the useful, plainspoken quality of the current page while changing the organizing idea:

> **From customer challenge to working concept.**

This is a positioning hypothesis for review, not a claim that every proposed capability or proof point is already a finished product.

## A. Homepage objective

### Five-second understanding

Within five seconds, a first-time business owner should understand:

> WelcomeMat Digital helps you see what a better customer experience could look like before you commit to building it.

The visitor should not need to understand the internal product names, the implementation stack, or whether the answer will be a website, assistant, quote flow, booking flow, or missed-call response.

### Intended feeling

The page should make WelcomeMat feel observant, practical, founder-led, and safe to explore. The visitor should feel that someone will listen to the real problem before recommending software, and that a small experiment is possible before a large commitment.

### Primary action

**Primary CTA:** `Share my business challenge` linking directly to `/concept`.

The CTA offers a low-pressure first step without implying that every visitor will receive a complimentary concept. The transition between this homepage language and the current Concept-intake headline must be reviewed during implementation so the visitor experiences one continuous journey rather than a confusing terminology change.

### BNI role

For the September 3 BNI meeting, the homepage is the credibility proof behind a short spoken introduction and a business-card QR code. It should let a member answer three questions quickly:

1. What does Sunil actually help with?
2. Is this relevant to someone I know?
3. What can I safely refer them to do next?

The page should support a discovery referral, not force a product or imply that a visitor has already been selected for the complimentary Concept initiative.

## B. Audience

The page should speak first to:

- **Small-business owners with unclear or fragmented customer experiences.** Their website, phone, Google listing, forms, messages, and follow-up may each work separately while the overall journey feels difficult.
- **Owners who know something is not working but do not know the solution.** They may feel the symptoms but not know whether they need better information, a form, a booking path, an assistant, or something else.
- **Referral partners.** Accountants, designers, marketers, photographers, business coaches, technology providers, and other trusted advisors may recognize a customer-experience problem and introduce WelcomeMat.
- **BNI members evaluating credibility and referral fit.** They need clear language, honest proof boundaries, a human point of contact, and a referral path that does not overpromise.

The overall positioning must not be limited to plumbers, cleaners, restaurants, or other local service businesses. Those examples can make the idea concrete, but the core problem is broader: a small organization has a customer journey that is confusing, slow, hard to access, or losing useful requests.

## C. Core positioning

### Messaging hierarchy

1. **Promise:** Turn a real customer challenge into a practical working concept.
2. **Method:** Discover the breakdown, prototype the smallest useful response, and review the concept together.
3. **Outcomes:** A clearer customer journey and a tangible concept that can be reviewed before a larger commitment.
4. **Proof:** Concept intake, Camellia Cleaning where permission and evidence are confirmed, FrontDesk AI capabilities, and Swoop as an explicitly labeled emerging prototype.

### Recommended hero

**Eyebrow:** A practical starting point for small businesses

**Headline:** See what a better customer experience could look like—before you commit to building it.

**Supporting statement:** Tell us where customers are getting stuck. We’ll explore the problem and shape a practical working concept—whether that means a clearer website, easier inquiries or booking, a multilingual AI assistant, or something else.

**Primary CTA:** Share my business challenge → `/concept`

**Supporting note beneath the CTA:** We’re currently selecting a small number of businesses for a complimentary concept. No pressure or commitment.

**Secondary CTA:** See how it works → the on-page Discovery → Prototype → Test section.

Use **From customer challenge to working concept.** as the memorable WelcomeMat Digital positioning line near the process section. It should support the hero rather than serve as the only customer-facing explanation.

### Discovery -> Prototype -> Test

- **Discover:** Listen to the owner and map how customers currently find, understand, contact, request, book, buy, or follow up. Identify the specific point of friction rather than assuming the answer is a new website.
- **Prototype:** Shape a small, reviewable concept using approved information. The result might be a page, multilingual assistant, quote flow, booking experience, missed-call response, or another focused tool.
- **Test:** Review the concept together, learn what feels useful or unclear, and refine what should happen next. A real-world pilot may be considered later when appropriate, but it is not part of the standard homepage promise.

“Test” must describe a proposed working method, not an unsupported claim that every project already includes formal user research or measured outcomes.

### How to describe WelcomeMat Digital

Recommended description:

> WelcomeMat Digital helps small businesses turn an unclear customer experience into a practical working concept. We start with the problem, then shape the right response.

Do not describe WelcomeMat Digital as a traditional web-design agency, a fixed website package, or a general-purpose AI consultancy. Calls, follow-up, communication, booking, inquiries, websites, multilingual experiences, AI assistants, quote flows, missed-call recovery, and other tools are possible responses to a discovered problem. They are not a package that every business must buy.

## F. WelcomeMat AI assistant experience

### Purpose and role

The assistant should be a guided front door to WelcomeMat Digital’s way of thinking, not a generic sales chatbot and not a replacement for the Concept intake. It demonstrates how WelcomeMat can shape a purposeful multilingual experience while helping visitors decide whether `/concept` is a sensible next step.

It should be visually integrated into the homepage, use the same typography, colors, language, spacing, and tone, and appear as one calm “Ask WelcomeMat” experience rather than an unrelated floating vendor widget.

### Recommended opening

> Hi, I’m the WelcomeMat guide. I can explain how we turn a customer-experience challenge into a working concept, or help you decide whether the complimentary Concept initiative is a fit. What would you like to explore?

### Quick-start options

- `How does WelcomeMat work?`
- `I know something is not working`
- `What is the complimentary Concept initiative?`
- `Show me an example`
- `Talk with Sunil`
- `Continue in Spanish` / `Continuar en español`
- `Continue in Portuguese` / `Continuar em português`

The exact language set should be approved before implementation. Spanish and Portuguese are already established in the existing product experience; additional languages should not be promised until content and review ownership exist.

### Questions it should answer

The assistant may answer:

- What WelcomeMat Digital does and who it may help.
- What “From customer challenge to working concept” means.
- How Discovery, Prototype, and Test work at a high level.
- What the Concept initiative is, who can express interest, what information is requested, and what is not guaranteed.
- What FrontDesk AI and Swoop demonstrate, using the labels `Working demonstration` and `Prototype in development`.
- How multilingual support can be shaped into a customer experience, while distinguishing that broader ability from the specific languages reviewed and production-tested for this assistant.
- Whether a visitor’s problem sounds like a possible discovery topic, without diagnosing or promising a solution.
- How to start at `/concept`, how to contact Sunil, and what happens after expressing interest.

### Explanation and routing behavior

The assistant should first reflect the visitor’s concern in plain language, then offer one of three routes:

1. `Start the complimentary Concept intake` -> `/concept`.
2. `Ask a few more discovery questions` -> continue in the assistant, without collecting sensitive information.
3. `Talk with Sunil` -> the approved contact path, after contact details are confirmed.

The assistant supports the primary CTA by reducing uncertainty. It must not turn the visitor into a separate funnel with a competing form or a long qualification interview.

### Multilingual behavior

Use a visible language control inside the assistant and allow the visitor to switch at any time. A language choice should persist for the current conversation and should not silently change the language of the surrounding page unless that page translation exists and is intentionally supported. Multilingual support should be framed as a broader WelcomeMat Digital design capability, not only as a chatbot feature.

Recommended behavior:

- Start in English unless the visitor chooses another language or the implementation has a reviewed, reliable language-detection path.
- Show language names in their native form where practical: English, Español, Português.
- Keep quick-start labels translated and reviewed, not machine-generated at render time.
- Reply in the selected language and preserve names, URLs, product labels, and maturity labels accurately.
- Publish only the specific assistant languages that have reviewed and production-tested content; do not imply unlimited language support.
- Offer an easy switch back to English and make the selected language clear to screen readers.
- Record language preference only when the user intentionally submits it through an approved flow. Do not treat a chat message as consent to store a profile.

### Boundaries and grounding

The assistant must never invent customer names, testimonials, metrics, pricing, selection decisions, implementation timelines, integrations, permissions, or outcomes. It must not claim that WelcomeMat Digital has built or tested something that is only a concept. It must not imply Microsoft affiliation, endorsement, or current employment.

Ground the assistant in an approved, versioned WelcomeMat content source containing:

- The reviewed homepage positioning and process language.
- `/concept` initiative rules, privacy wording, selection limits, and intake link.
- Approved facts about FrontDesk AI, Swoop, and any public examples.
- Approved contact and handoff details.
- A clear list of unknowns and prohibited claims.

The existing chat architecture is business-specific: it uses a business prompt and explicit language handling for a customer site. A homepage WelcomeMat assistant should not reuse a customer’s prompt or data. Before implementation, decide whether to add a separate neutral WelcomeMat assistant prompt/route or create an explicit WelcomeMat business configuration with isolated data and instructions. This is an implementation dependency, not something the brief assumes is already solved.

### Failure and fallback behavior

- If the assistant cannot answer from its approved source, say so plainly: “I don’t have a reliable answer for that yet.”
- Offer `/concept`, the approved contact path, or a request to ask a narrower question.
- If the service is unavailable, keep the primary CTA and a normal contact link fully usable.
- Never fabricate an answer to keep the conversation moving.
- Show a short privacy note before or beside the first message, and a clearer disclosure before collecting any information.

### Mobile and accessibility

The assistant should remain user-initiated and open in a stable, full-width mobile panel or accessible dialog rather than covering the primary CTA. It must support keyboard navigation, visible focus, Escape to close, screen-reader labels, readable contrast, reduced-motion preferences, text resizing, and a non-chat path to `/concept`. Quick-start options must be real buttons, not only decorative pills. Error and loading states need text equivalents.

### Automatic opening recommendation

**Recommendation: do not open automatically.** A user-initiated assistant respects attention, avoids covering the BNI QR landing experience, reduces surprise on mobile, and keeps the `/concept` CTA clearly primary. A quiet “Ask WelcomeMat” prompt can be visible after the hero and near the process section.

## G. Visual direction

### Direction

Use a warm, editorial field-notes quality: clear typography, generous but practical spacing, restrained color, and evidence shown as working artifacts. The page should feel like a thoughtful founder’s workshop translated into a polished digital experience, not a software marketplace.

- **Typography:** Keep the current purposeful pairing of a readable sans serif for body text and a distinctive serif for headlines if it remains legible and performant. Use type hierarchy to create confidence, not oversized hero text. Avoid default all-purpose startup typography and avoid novelty fonts.
- **Color:** Retain a restrained navy, teal, warm gold, and light neutral foundation where it supports brand continuity. Use teal for action and gold sparingly for emphasis. Do not turn every section into a gradient or use purple as the default AI signal.
- **Spacing and layout:** Use a clear reading column with occasional evidence-led split layouts. Keep sections full-width and unframed where possible; reserve cards for individual examples, the assistant, and genuinely framed tools. Do not put cards inside cards.
- **Photography and imagery:** Prefer one real, approved founder photo and real product or workflow screenshots over generic business stock. Candidate or customer images require permission. If no approved photo is ready, use typography and interface evidence rather than a fabricated portrait or decorative illustration.
- **Screenshots and demonstrations:** Show a small number of annotated, current screenshots or a short interactive demonstration. Label what is live, prototype, or illustrative. Never show a screenshot that implies a customer outcome not supported by the underlying source.
- **Icons:** Use a restrained, consistent icon set only where it improves scanning. Avoid an icon for every feature. Simple arrows, language controls, and status labels are enough for v1.
- **Motion:** Use a small page-load reveal and subtle process transitions, with reduced-motion support. Motion should help establish sequence, not make the page feel like a product demo template.
- **Desktop and mobile:** Make the first screen clear at mobile widths, especially for QR referrals. Stack process and proof sections predictably, keep the CTA reachable, and ensure the assistant does not obscure content or require horizontal scrolling.
- **Founder presence:** Let Sunil’s first-person story and one real image or direct contact path carry human credibility. Avoid turning the homepage into a resume or a founder bio wall.

### Patterns to avoid

- Excessive gradients, glowing borders, floating blobs, or floating glass cards.
- Generic AI robot imagery or synthetic “futuristic” scenes.
- Excessive feature icons and repeated three-column marketing grids.
- Unsupported statistics, testimonials, logos, customer counts, or conversion claims.
- Overly polished stock-business imagery that does not show the actual work.
- Large amounts of generic marketing copy that say “transform,” “revolutionize,” or “unlock” without a concrete action.
- Visual styles that make unrelated sections look generated from separate templates.
- A product-pricing grid that makes FrontDesk AI, Swoop, and Concept look like competing packages.

### Implementation review requirement

Before publication, review desktop and mobile screenshots at representative widths. Visual QA must specifically check whether the page feels handcrafted, coherent, and credible rather than AI-generated or assembled from generic landing-page patterns. Check the transition from the homepage CTA to the `/concept` headline, evidence labels, multilingual content, assistant states, spacing, and mobile reading order.

## I. CTA and conversion model

### CTA model

- **Single primary CTA:** `Share my business challenge` -> `/concept`.
- **Secondary CTA:** `See how the process works` -> on-page process section. A secondary `Talk with Sunil` link can appear in the founder/final sections.
- **Proof links:** FrontDesk and Swoop links should be quiet, contextual “See this example” actions rather than equal-weight product CTAs.

The primary CTA should link directly to `/concept`, not to a new intermediate quiz. The existing intake already captures enough context for a first pass and includes privacy, no-pressure, selection, and materials language. A new lightweight discovery entry point would add implementation and conversion risk before September 3. The current `/concept` page uses its own Concept-intake terminology, so implementation must review the transition from `Share my business challenge` to the intake-page headline and supporting copy for continuity.

### After interest is expressed

The visitor should see the existing confirmation that the information was received and that WelcomeMat will review it before contacting them if selected. Internally, the inquiry should remain subject to the existing review process; the homepage must not promise a meeting, selection, prototype, or delivery date.

### Referral and QR behavior

Use a stable production URL for the card QR code. The business-card QR should point to the homepage root, `https://welcomematdigital.com/`, unless a later reviewed decision changes it. Make the primary `/concept` CTA visible in the first viewport. Test the QR from a printed proof and on a phone before ordering cards. If later attribution is needed, add it without changing the stable visible destination or creating a second funnel.

## K. Risks and decisions

### Claims requiring validation

- Whether “complimentary Concept” selection limits, timing, and eligibility remain current.
- Which FrontDesk capabilities are safe to demonstrate publicly, and whether “multilingual” needs a narrower supported-language statement.
- Whether Swoop should be shown publicly before its next reliable demonstration and operating status are confirmed.
- What Camellia Cleaning work, screenshots, business name, reviews, and outcomes may be shown publicly.
- Whether the proposed founder copy accurately describes Sunil’s background.
- Which contact details are approved for public use.

### Privacy and data risks

- The homepage assistant must not use a customer business prompt, customer records, or Concept submissions as general knowledge.
- Chat transcripts, language preference, and contact details need an explicit retention and access decision before implementation.
- The assistant should avoid collecting detailed business information in chat when `/concept` is the approved intake.
- Concept submissions remain private unless the owner gives permission to share them.
- Candidate names and materials must not become public proof by implication.

### Positioning and maturity risks

- Over-positioning unfinished prototypes could damage trust with BNI referrals.
- Presenting three products equally would recreate the current disconnected-product problem.
- Broadening beyond local service businesses could become vague unless examples remain concrete.
- A new assistant can distract from the primary CTA if it opens automatically or becomes a second intake form.
- Reusing the existing business-specific assistant architecture without isolation could expose the wrong grounding or behavior.
- The current homepage and Swoop pages contain older product/pricing language; implementation must reconcile those labels rather than copy them uncritically.

### Decisions required before implementation

The following are approved direction for the brief and should guide implementation:

- Use **WelcomeMat Digital** as the formal brand, **WelcomeMat** as conversational shorthand, and `welcomematdigital.com` as the domain.
- Use the reviewed hero, `Share my business challenge` → `/concept` CTA, and the six-section editorial architecture.
- Use **From customer challenge to working concept.** near the process section as the memorable positioning line.
- Define Test as reviewing the concept together and refining what should happen next; do not promise a pilot or formal research.
- Use the maturity labels: Concept intake — Available now; FrontDesk AI — Working demonstration; Swoop — Prototype in development; Camellia — Business example, only after explicit permission and factual review; Future candidate concepts — Not public until completed and approved.
- Treat multilingual support as a broader WelcomeMat Digital capability, while publishing only assistant languages that are reviewed and production-tested.
- Use the first-person founder copy and the screenshot-based visual QA requirement.
- Use the homepage root as the business-card QR destination unless a later reviewed decision changes it.
